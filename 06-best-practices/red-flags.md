# Governance Red Flags

**Conditions that, if present, indicate the AI governance framework is failing regardless of how the reporting reads. Each flag names the doctrine it breaches and the first remedial step.**

Flags 1 to 16 take their subjects, order and four groups from the governance red flags in AICD and the UTS Human Technology Institute, *A Director's Guide to AI Governance*, Version 2 (June 2026), licensed CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, and restate each in the author's words against the three doctrines; flags 17 to 20 are the author's own.

## Strategy flags

1. **Tools before purpose.** The count of AI tools in use keeps rising and nobody can show, tool by tool, which strategic objective each one serves or what it was expected to return. Breach: Slow AI; the tools are deployed before they are governed. First step: no new approvals until every tool in use is on the register with its objective and its owner.
2. **Initiatives without a strategic parent.** Live AI projects cannot be traced to anything the board approved as strategy; they exist because a competitor or a vendor moved. Breach: Informed Intent; nobody authorized a purpose. First step: every live initiative names the objective it serves within the quarter or is wound down.
3. **Appetite unstated.** The board has never said how much AI risk it will accept, or how that limit sits with the appetite, register and framework it already applies to everything else. Breach: Final Liability; owners are named against limits that do not exist. First step: one board session that ends in a written, differentiated appetite statement.
4. **Capability assumed.** The investment plan presumes people, skills and systems the organization has never counted. Breach: Informed Intent; the authorizer lacks the Knowledge of what can be delivered. First step: the baseline exercise described in [Strategy and risk appetite](../handbook.md#strategy-and-risk-appetite).

## Structure flags

5. **Adoption without a gate.** AI arrives through procurement, plug-ins and personal accounts, and at no point does anyone ask whether it needs controls of its own. Breach: Slow AI. First step: one approval gate, then an inventory of everything that came in around it.
6. **No name at the top.** Asked who answers for AI governance, management offers a committee, a function or several names. Breach: Final Liability, at the root. First step: name the executive this quarter, then cascade.
7. **Agents outside the framework.** Systems that act, not only advise, are running or planned, and the risk framework treats them as one more category of software. Breach: Informed Intent. First step: an agent inventory, then an authorization for each stating its purpose, authority, boundaries and exit.
8. **Bad news travels slowly.** The board hears of AI problems late, second-hand or not at all. Breach: Final Liability; the power to intervene is empty without the information to use it. First step: escalation triggers with named owners, tested against the last three incidents.

## Practice flags

9. **A risk framework that never says AI.** Read end to end, the enterprise risk framework does not mention artificial intelligence. First step: fold the eight-area [risk taxonomy](../handbook.md#a-working-risk-taxonomy) into the existing framework, not a parallel one.
10. **Nothing assessed before go-live.** An AI system can reach production without anyone having asked what it could get wrong and for whom. Breach: Slow AI; a system nobody assessed cannot be explained or audited. First step: a risk-tiered assessment attached to the approval gate.
11. **Agents without controls.** Agents are in production with no documented task appetite, no access limits, no action log and no kill switch anyone has tested. Breach: Informed Intent, in production. First step: suspend or checkpoint the agents until authorizations exist.
12. **Policies written for a world without AI.** Data governance, privacy, cyber and procurement policies predate the organization's use of AI and nobody has reopened them since. First step: a dated review cycle with AI-specific triggers.

## Enabler flags

13. **The affected are unknown.** Management cannot say who, inside or outside the organization, its AI deployments affect, or how. Breach: Final Liability; outcomes attach to people the organization has not identified. First step: impact mapping for the five largest deployments, employees first.
14. **The data is unknown.** There is no inventory of the data the AI systems draw on and no measured view of its quality. Breach: Slow AI; what a system was fed cannot be explained. First step: the inventory comes before further AI investment.
15. **Users untrained.** Staff use AI tools widely with little or no instruction in how those tools fail. First step: training tied to tool access, with completion a prerequisite.
16. **Infrastructure unexamined.** Nobody can describe the compute, integration and running cost that current and planned AI workloads require, or how that cost scales. First step: management assurance on current and planned workloads, with the non-linear scaling case modeled.

## Meta-flags

17. **Fluent reporting, no names.** Board papers discuss "the AI," "the model" and "the vendor" as actors; no human is named as owning any outcome. Breach: Final Liability, disguised as diction. First step: return the paper.
18. **Irreversibility normalized.** Nobody can say how a given system would be switched off, what would replace it or what exit would cost, and this is treated as normal. Breach: Slow AI. First step: exit documentation as a condition of the next renewal.
19. **The register is a souvenir.** An AI inventory exists but has no refresh process, lists departed employees as owners or omits embedded and shadow use. First step: refresh process with a named owner, then a reconciliation against network monitoring.
20. **Perfect record.** No AI use case has ever been rejected at the gate, no deployment has ever been rolled back and no initiative has ever been retired. A governance system that never says no is not operating; it is countersigning. First step: audit the gate.
