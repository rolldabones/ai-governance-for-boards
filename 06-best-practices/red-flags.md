# Governance Red Flags

**Conditions that, if present, indicate the AI governance framework is failing regardless of how the reporting reads. Each flag names the doctrine it breaches and the first remedial step.**

## Strategy flags

1. **Tool accumulation without alignment review.** The organization is adopting a large number of AI tools without reviewing alignment with strategy or potential return on investment. Breach: Slow AI (adoption outpacing governance). First step: freeze new approvals until the register and strategy filter operate.
2. **AI initiatives disconnected from core strategy.** Projects exist because the market moved, not because the strategy called for them. First step: require every live initiative to state the strategic objective it serves or enter wind-down.
3. **No AI-specific risk appetite.** The board has not considered an AI risk appetite consistent with the organization's overall appetite, risk register and risk management framework. Breach: the frame within which Final Liability owners operate does not exist. First step: a board session producing a differentiated appetite statement.
4. **Resourcing blindness.** No complete understanding of current resources and internal expertise, or whether they will support planned AI investments. First step: the baseline exercise described in [Strategy and risk appetite](../03-operating-discipline/strategy-and-risk-appetite.md).

## Structure flags

5. **Ad hoc adoption.** AI is adopted in an unplanned way without consideration of whether specific governance processes and risk controls are needed. First step: institute the approval gate, then inventory what bypassed it.
6. **Unattributable governance.** It is not clear who has responsibility for AI governance in the organization. Breach: Final Liability, at the root. First step: name the executive this quarter, then cascade.
7. **Agentic risk invisible in the framework.** Agentic AI risks are not highlighted in the risk management framework and corresponding controls, even though agents are deployed or imminent. Breach: Informed Intent. First step: agent inventory, then authorization instruments for each.
8. **Escalation failure.** AI risks are not reported to the board, or are not escalated in a timely way. First step: defined escalation triggers with named owners, tested against the last three incidents.

## Practice flags

9. **ERM silence on AI.** The organization's risk management framework does not address AI risks at all. First step: integrate the eight-area [risk taxonomy](../02-board-mandate/risk-taxonomy.md) into the existing framework, not a parallel one.
10. **No assessment process.** There is no process to assess and evaluate the risks of any AI-enabled application or system. First step: risk-tiered assessment attached to the approval gate.
11. **Agents without controls.** Agentic AI systems have been adopted without rigorous risk identification and controls: no documented task appetite, no access limits, no action logs, no tested kill switch. Breach: Informed Intent, in production. First step: suspend or checkpoint the agents until authorizations exist.
12. **Stale control estate.** Data governance and privacy controls and policies have not been reviewed for fitness against AI systems. First step: a dated review cycle with AI-specific triggers.

## Enabler flags

13. **Stakeholder impact unaccounted.** Management is unable to provide an account of how AI implementation and use will impact key stakeholders. First step: impact mapping for the top five deployments, employees first.
14. **No data stocktake.** There is no data inventory and limited understanding of internal data quality. Breach: the substrate of every other practice is missing. First step: the data stocktake precedes further AI investment.
15. **Untrained workforce.** No or limited training and support for employees on AI system use and risks, in an organization with significant adoption. First step: training tied to tool access, following the proven pattern of making completion a prerequisite.
16. **Infrastructure ignorance.** Weak understanding of the technological infrastructure necessary to support AI implementation, including how compute demand and consumption cost scale. First step: management assurance on current and planned workloads, with the non-linear scaling case modeled.

## Meta-flags

17. **Fluent reporting, no names.** Board papers discuss "the AI," "the model" and "the vendor" as actors; no human is named as owning any outcome. Breach: Final Liability, disguised as diction. First step: return the paper.
18. **Irreversibility normalized.** Nobody can say how a given system would be switched off, what would replace it or what exit would cost, and this is treated as normal. Breach: Slow AI. First step: exit documentation as a condition of the next renewal.
19. **The register is a souvenir.** An AI inventory exists but has no refresh process, lists departed employees as owners or omits embedded and shadow use. First step: refresh process with a named owner, then a reconciliation against network monitoring.
20. **Perfect record.** No AI use case has ever been rejected at the gate, no deployment has ever been rolled back and no initiative has ever been retired. A governance system that never says no is not operating; it is countersigning. First step: audit the gate.
